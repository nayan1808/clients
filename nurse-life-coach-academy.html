01 · Situation

The two ways high-ticket funnels die

Nurse Life Coach Academy certifies nurses as life coaches, a $4,000 decision a burned-out nurse makes carefully. High-ticket education funnels on Meta usually die one of two ways: creative that speaks to everyone and moves no one, or a CAC that eats the margin. This account risked both.

02 · Hypothesis

"Nurses" is not one audience, and generic creative is the real CAC problem

A nurse of 20 years staring down burnout, a younger nurse looking for a side income, and a nurse already coaching informally respond to different stories. If each segment got its own creative angle and its own landing page, conversion rates should rise enough to hold CAC under control at scale. If the segments performed the same, the problem would be the offer, not the funnel.

03 · Method

One funnel per story, tested continuously

  • Segment-level creative testing: each segment got its own creative angles, tested continuously rather than in one-off batches.
  • Persona-matched landing pages: the page continued the exact story the ad started, per segment, instead of routing everyone to one generic page.
  • Funnel structure: rebuilt from cold intro through enrollment conversation, so spend could scale without the usual audience fatigue collapse.
Finding At $4,000 a seat, the ad's job isn't to sell. It's to make the right nurse feel found.

04 · Result

The economics held while scaling

Six months of spend at $293K produced $1.47M in closed-won revenue: a 5.05x ROAS with CAC held at $829 against a $4,000 average order value. Holding those numbers while scaling is the part most high-ticket accounts never manage; the segment structure is what made it possible.
