Portfolio/Makobi USA

Holding 07 · D2C Apparel eCommerce · Shopify

The unglamorous fixes behind a 23% new-customer jump

Streetwear eCommerce on Shopify lives or dies on its product feed and its repeat rate. The bet was that the account's ceiling wasn't creative or budget, it was plumbing.

01 · Situation

A deep catalog with quiet handbrakes on

Makobi USA is an LA-based streetwear brand selling direct on Shopify, with one of the deepest Big & Tall ranges in urban fashion. The paid account had the classic eCommerce problem set: feed errors quietly capping catalog performance, acquisition and retention blurred into the same campaigns, and no email program catching the buyers the ads brought in.

02 · Hypothesis

The ceiling is infrastructure, not creative

Catalog ads can only sell what the feed shows them, and this feed was showing wrong products at wrong prices. If the feed was cleaned, acquisition and retention split into separately measured campaigns, and email flows built to catch first-time buyers, revenue should move without new creative or bigger budgets. If it didn't move, then the account had a demand problem worth solving differently.

03 · Method

Fix the plumbing, then separate the jobs

  • Product feed fixes: cleaned the Shopify-to-Meta feed so catalog ads showed the right products at the right prices.
  • Catalog campaigns: restructured dynamic product campaigns around the fixed feed for new-customer acquisition.
  • Retention campaigns: separated repeat-purchase audiences from acquisition, each measured and budgeted on its own job.
  • Email flows: built nurture flows in Klaviyo and Omnisend to turn first-time buyers into repeat customers without more ad spend.

04 · Result

The infrastructure bet paid inside a month

New-customer acquisition revenue grew 23% within one month of the changes, with no new creative spend required. The retention layer meant those customers had somewhere to go after the first order.
