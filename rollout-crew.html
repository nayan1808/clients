01 · Situation

A practical category, allergic to SaaS playbooks

The Rollout Crew operates in construction technology, where buyers are practical, sales cycles run long, and generic SaaS playbooks miss. The company needed a paid program that produced sales-qualified pipeline, not form fills, and it needed a plan the internal team could run themselves.

02 · Hypothesis

In this category, the strategy is the deliverable, not the daily optimization

Construction-tech search demand is specific and high-intent; the risk wasn't targeting, it was structure and discipline. If the channel mix, campaign architecture, and budget thresholds were designed correctly and documented clearly, a capable internal team should be able to execute them and produce SQL-grade pipeline without a strategist living in the account. If that failed, the problem would be the plan, not the people.

03 · Method

Architecture, documented to be executable by someone other than me

  • Channel mix: where Google Ads earns its budget in this category and what each campaign type's job is.
  • Campaign structure: how search intent maps to campaigns and ad groups, with ABM-style targeting layered on top for named accounts.
  • Budget plan: spend allocation with clear thresholds for scaling up or cutting.
  • Handoff: the internal team ran execution against the documented plan.
Finding A strategy that only works with its author in the room isn't a strategy. It's a dependency.

04 · Result

The plan survived the handoff

Executing the documented architecture, the team turned $50K in Google Ads spend into 52 SQLs and $594K in closed-won revenue, with $1.5M in open pipeline after seven months. The spend-to-closed ratio came in near 12:1 before counting the open pipeline.
