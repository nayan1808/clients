Winning brand search back from Zoom, Otter, and Fireflies

People searching for Fathom by name were landing on competitors. The first question wasn't how to grow, it was why the company's own demand cost $50 a signup to capture.

01 · Situation

A contested category, starting with your own name

Fathom is a top-5 AI notetaker on G2 in a category where Zoom, Otter, and Fireflies contest every keyword, including each other's brand names. When I took over the Google Search program, Fathom's own brand terms showed under 10% impression share, and brand signups cost $50 each.

02 · Hypothesis

Brand demand is being priced by competitors, and non-brand can't work until that stops

Auction insights suggested the problem wasn't demand, it was ownership: competitors were setting the price of Fathom's own branded traffic. If brand defense was rebuilt market by market, signup costs on brand should collapse, and only then would non-brand expansion have a stable base to build on. Launching non-brand first would have poured budget into a leaking account.

03 · Method

Defense first, then offense

  • Brand defense: overhauled brand campaigns across four markets (US, UK, CA, AU), rebuilding structure, match types, and ad copy market by market.
  • Non-brand entry: with the base secured, launched non-brand search into head terms contested by companies with far bigger budgets.
  • Message fit as the wedge: ad copy written separately for sales ICPs and marketing ICPs, matched to what each actually wants from a notetaker, instead of trying to outbid.
  • Both sides of the click: worked directly with Fathom's Marketing Director on website page changes to lift conversion rates.
Finding If a competitor owns your brand keyword, they set the price of your own demand.

04 · Result

Both stages confirmed

Within three weeks, brand impression share sat at 80–90% in every market and cost per signup dropped from $50 to under $10. Non-brand then hit conversion rates up to 6% and cost per signup fell from $210 to $80 within two months, in one of the most contested categories in SaaS.
